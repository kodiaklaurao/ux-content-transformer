# Style

## Vocabulary
Since the platform is translated into German (and to keep the possibility of translation into other languages in the future) we need to use simple words and basic sentence structures.

- Avoid hedge words: "maybe", "possibly", "potentially", "can".
- Keep things light, but professional.
- Use motivational words, like: accomplish, action, challenge, commitment, courage, dare, determination, drive, envision, excellence, focus, goals, possibilities, potential, succeed.
- Use positive language.
- Avoid jargon wherever possible. If you must use acronyms, spell them out the first time they come up in the document, and then as acronyms in subsequent mentions.
- Be clear about the flexibility of our product, but don't overpromise or overstate.
  - For example, use "configurable" instead of "tailored" or "customizable."

## Numbers
- Use numerals — it makes skimming easier.
- For currency use 123,456.78 format.
- For dates use yyyy-MM-dd format.

## Verbosity
- Use active voice and simple sentence structure.
- Be accurate and concise — respect the user's time and intelligence.

## Grammar
- Use contractions, e.g. "we're" instead of "we are."
- Use simple and direct sentences with active voice.
- Use present and future tenses.
- Use present continuous tense and phrasing that implies curiosity.

| Good example | Do not use |
|---|---|
| We're gathering performance data from your suppliers. | Data is being gathered from your suppliers. |

## Punctuation
- Use exclamation marks sparingly.
- Put spaces — around — em dashes.
- Only use the ampersand (&) in headings and subheadings.
- Use Oxford comma for clarity.

| Good example | Do not use |
|---|---|
| Kodiak Hub helps procurement, sustainability, and quality teams work smarter together. | Kodiak Hub helps procurement, sustainability and quality teams work smarter together. |

**Why it's correct**
- The Oxford comma (after "sustainability") makes the list clear and unambiguous.
- It prevents the reader from mistakenly thinking "sustainability and quality teams" is one combined group.
- This aligns with professional writing standards and the clarity principle in UX and brand communication.

**Why it's incorrect**
- Without the comma after "sustainability," the sentence can be misread as if "sustainability and quality" is a single unit or department.
- It introduces momentary confusion, something UX writing should always avoid.

## Capitalization
Use sentence case (capitalize the first word only) for:
- Headings
- Subheadings
- Buttons

### Module names
- Use a lower-case bolded prefix and an upper-case suffix.
  - e.g. in(SITE)

### Feature names
Some feature names are branded. For example, we capitalize Smart Actions.

# Headings
Headings and subheadings are titles and subtitles that refer to sections of the interface. Headings appear at the top of the page, pane, or modal.

Headings and subheadings should be:

**Informative and descriptive**
- Highlight the most important concept or piece of information for users.
- Help users understand what they'll find in the product area, or what they must do.

| Good example | Do not use |
|---|---|
| Select suppliers | Who is monitored |

- Correct: instructs the user on what they must do in this step.
- Incorrect: the notification is not monitoring a person, it's monitoring a company. It's also phrased like a question — we should be guiding the user, not asking them a question.

**Concise and scannable**
- Use simple, clear language.
- Keep headings to a short, single sentence.
- Avoid using punctuation such as periods, commas, or semicolons.
- Write in sentence case (capitalize the first word and proper nouns only).
- Whether or not to use articles ("the," "a," "an") in headings and subheadings depends on the type of message.
